Mission phase

  • Launch and Earth orbit (16 Jul 1969, 13:32:00 – 16:16:16 UTC) Apollo 11 lifts off and reaches Earth orbit about 12 minutes later. The crew and Mission Control check the spacecraft for one and a half orbits before heading for the Moon.
  • Coast to the Moon (16 Jul 1969, 16:16:16 – 19 Jul 1969, 17:21:50 UTC) Pushed out of Earth orbit by the Saturn V's third stage, the spacecraft coasts for about three days to the Moon.
  • Lunar orbit (19 Jul 1969, 17:21:50 – 22 Jul 1969, 04:55:42 UTC) Columbia circles the Moon 30 times, while Eagle goes down to the surface and comes back.
  • Coast back to Earth (22 Jul 1969, 04:55:42 – 24 Jul 1969, 16:50:35 UTC) The crew coasts home for about two and a half days, ending in splashdown in the Pacific Ocean.

Saturn V

  • Launch (16 Jul 1969, 13:32:00 UTC) A Saturn V rocket lifts Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin and Michael Collins off Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center, Florida, at 9:32 a.m. local time.
  • Translunar injection (16 Jul 1969, 16:16:16 UTC) The rocket's third stage fires again for about six minutes, sending the spacecraft out of Earth orbit towards the Moon.

Columbia

  • Lunar orbit insertion (19 Jul 1969, 17:21:50 UTC) Behind the Moon and out of radio contact with Earth, Columbia's engine fires to slow the spacecraft into lunar orbit.
  • Collins flies Columbia alone (20 Jul 1969, 17:44:00 – 21 Jul 1969, 21:35:00 UTC) Michael Collins orbits the Moon alone in the command module while Armstrong and Aldrin are on the surface, cut off from everyone each time he passes behind the Moon.
  • Trans-Earth injection (22 Jul 1969, 04:55:42 UTC) Again behind the Moon, Columbia's engine fires for about two and a half minutes to send the crew home.
  • Splashdown (24 Jul 1969, 16:50:35 UTC) Columbia lands in the Pacific Ocean under its parachutes, and the crew is recovered by the aircraft carrier USS Hornet. The mission lasted 8 days, 3 hours and 18 minutes.

Eagle

  • Eagle undocks (20 Jul 1969, 17:44:00 UTC) With Armstrong and Aldrin aboard, the lunar module Eagle separates from Columbia to begin its descent.
  • Powered descent (20 Jul 1969, 20:05 – 20:17:40 UTC) Eagle's descent engine fires for the last 12 minutes down to the surface.
  • 1202 program alarm (20 Jul 1969, 20:10 UTC) The overloaded guidance computer raises a series of alarms during the descent. Mission Control decides within seconds that it is safe to continue.
  • The Eagle has landed (20 Jul 1969, 20:17:40 UTC) Armstrong steers past a field of boulders and lands in the Sea of Tranquility with little fuel to spare: "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."
  • Eagle on the Moon (20 Jul 1969, 20:17:40 – 21 Jul 1969, 17:54:00 UTC) Eagle stays at Tranquility Base for about 21 and a half hours, including the moonwalk and a rest period before the return to orbit.
  • Lunar liftoff (21 Jul 1969, 17:54:00 UTC) Eagle's ascent stage lifts off, using the descent stage as its launch pad and leaving it on the Moon.
  • Eagle docks with Columbia (21 Jul 1969, 21:35:00 UTC) The ascent stage rejoins Columbia in lunar orbit, and Armstrong and Aldrin move back into the command module with their samples.
  • Eagle jettisoned (21 Jul 1969, 23:41:31 UTC) The empty ascent stage is released and left in lunar orbit.

Moonwalk

  • Moonwalk (21 Jul 1969, 02:39:33 – 05:11:13 UTC) From opening Eagle's hatch to closing it again, Armstrong and Aldrin spend about two and a half hours outside, collecting rock samples, setting up experiments and planting a flag.
  • One small step (21 Jul 1969, 02:56:15 UTC) Neil Armstrong becomes the first person to step onto the Moon: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind."
  • Aldrin steps onto the Moon (21 Jul 1969, 03:14 UTC) Buzz Aldrin follows Armstrong down the ladder and describes the view as "magnificent desolation".

Sources

Times are in UTC, as given by these sources. Where they give a time to the second, so does the timeline; the start of powered descent, the 1202 alarm and Aldrin's first step are given to the minute.

  • Apollo 11, Wikipedia: launch, lunar orbit insertion, undocking, landing, the moonwalk, lunar liftoff, docking, jettison of the ascent stage, trans-Earth injection and splashdown.
  • Apollo 11 Lunar Surface Journal, NASA's annotated transcripts of the landing and the moonwalk, timed from launch:
    • The Landing: powered descent, the 1202 program alarm and the landing.
    • One Small Step: the hatch opening and the first steps of Armstrong and Aldrin.
    • EASEP Deployment and Closeout: the hatch closing at the end of the moonwalk.
    • The Return to Orbit: lunar liftoff.
